Sutton and Barto from Scratch 5: Mars Lander from Flight Logs

How it was built

Part 1: The Lander, Its Autopilots and the Flight Logs

Simulate a one-dimensional Mars landing with altitude, vertical velocity and fuel, define the cautious autopilot that is in service and the fast autopilot mission control wants to try, record flight logs, estimate landing rates by flying, and compute the exact landing probability of every state by dynamic programming.

Part 2: Features for a Continuous Lander

Turn the lander's telemetry into feature vectors four ways from section 9.5: a polynomial basis on the raw numbers, a Fourier cosine basis, radial basis functions and tile coding, and set the step size with the rule of section 9.6.

Part 3: Learning the Cautious Autopilot's Value On-Policy

Learn the in-service autopilot's landing probability from its own logs with semi-gradient TD(0) and gradient Monte Carlo, the data-efficient LSTD of section 9.8, memory-based kernel regression from sections 9.9-9.10, and interest and emphasis on the book's Example 9.4.

Part 4: The Incident: Off-Policy Divergence

Evaluate the fast autopilot from the cautious autopilot's logs: compute importance ratios, run the expected off-policy semi-gradient TD update and watch the weights explode, take the deadly triad apart one leg at a time, and reproduce the two minimal counterexamples of section 11.2: Tsitsiklis and Van Roy's two states and Baird's seven.

Part 5: Geometry, Gradient-TD, Emphatic-TD and the Go/No-Go Call

Measure the four objectives of section 11.4 (VE, BE, PBE, TDE), show on the A-presplit example why minimising the Bellman error is the wrong fix, then repair off-policy learning with TDC/GTD2 and emphatic TD, compare true online TD(lambda) with TD(lambda), and turn every estimate into a go/no-go decision for the fast autopilot.
